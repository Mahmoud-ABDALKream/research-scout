This is Research Scout, my AI Fluency capstone.

It is a personal agent that pre-qualifies React and product roles for me: healthcare, e-commerce, junior-friendly, Egypt or remote.

I used to spend half an hour a day scanning LinkedIn, Wuzzuf, and Upwork. The agent gathers listings, reads the job pages, scores each one, and filters the weak fits. I still click Apply. It never submits an application. That is the first thing to understand: ranking is the job. Submitting is mine.

Watch the live dashboard. I am clicking Run Scout now. No slides. This is the real app.

Step one is gather. About twenty-six sources run in parallel: Wuzzuf, LinkedIn, Indeed, Bayt, GulfTalent, Upwork, Mostaql, RemoteOK, Remotive, The Muse, We Work Remotely, Hacker News, and others. Some sources return jobs. Some fail. That is expected. When a board is down, we do not fake listings.

Step two is read. The agent fetches the actual job description from each top URL. If the page is blocked, it scores from the snippet instead of inventing text.

Step three is score. Each role is scored out of nine: skill match, domain match, seniority fit, and location fit. A junior React role in Egypt should sit at five or above. A Staff Engineer in San Francisco should not. Watch the live scores appear.

Now the design decision I would make again.

The agent is not allowed to apply. There is no HTTP call that submits an application to a job board. Ranking is allowed. Auto-apply is not. That is a product boundary, not a missing feature.

The other half of that decision is the seniority penalty. On the first run, a Senior Front-End role scored five out of nine because React and healthcare matched. I am a student. Five is the threshold, so that senior role would have landed in my digest.

So I added four mechanical signals: an LLM too-senior flag, the word senior in the title, six-plus years in the job description, and lead or staff in the title. The penalty is two to four points, capped at minus four. Here is the eval panel: six tests, six pass. That is mechanical, not a vibe.

Honest limitation: a lot of boards block the reader. LinkedIn and Upwork often return Cloudflare or a login wall. Those jobs get a low-signal flag or a zero, and they fall out of the digest. I do not invent a job description I could not read. The cost is coverage, especially freelance gigs. A future version would use official APIs or RSS, not a stealth scraper.

If I upload my C V, search and scoring follow those skills. Here is an apply kit: an English pitch and an Arabic why. I copy it, open the listing, and apply myself. The guardrail is still on screen: the agent never auto-applies.
